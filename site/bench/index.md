# Bench

Size, accuracy, closed-loop game results and latency of the published models, from `site/public/bench.json`:

- the game models, s3-pico (the default), s1-pico and s2-nano;
- the Doom fine-tune s2-nano-doom, listed from the manifest without metrics;
- the two models for any question, s4-micro and s5-lite, whose held-out scores are in the table below the bench;
- s6-nano and s6-micro (the second version), which do both, listed from the manifest: their results are [below](#s6-results);
- s6-secrets-pico, a secret detector that answers only its four questions about a text and fails on real files (it flags 59.8% of clean real files at p ≥ 0.9), listed from the manifest: its results are [below](#s6-results) too, and the browser button measures it on game states it was never trained for, which only says how fast it runs;
- the five s6 models that failed their gates, listed from the manifest with **failed** in the gate column: each failed its gate; its file is published as a result (owner's decision of 15/11/2026). s6-micro-v1, s6-secrets-verify-pico and s6-route-nano are timed by the button (the last two on game states they were never trained for); s6-ear-nano hears audio and s6-cast-pico forecasts series, so the button does not time them. Their results are [below](#s6-results).

Accuracy is measured on the held-out test split without the Doom questions (25,319 questions): for s1-pico and s2-nano with NLL and ECE from `runs/s1-pico-g2/report.md` and `runs/s2-nano-g2/report.md` (written by `sky-train eval` and `sky-train s2-eval`), for s3-pico from `BENCH.md` (s3 section). The games are 30 seeded `sky-arena` episodes per game (`runs/ship-*.arena.json`). Native latency is the `sky-arena` median per Flappy decision on the build machine; WASM latency is the median per Flappy frame (2 questions + physics) under Node until you measure your own. A value shown as **n/a** was not measured for that model. Use the button below to measure latency in your own browser.

## s6 results

Nine s6 experiments have a pre-registration with its gate. Eight of them trained and tested nine models (s6-micro twice): four pass and are published (s6-nano, s6-secrets-pico on its generated bench, the second version of s6-micro, on a second look at the same test records, and s6-ear2-nano, the dedicated end-of-turn student of Smart Turn), five fail: each failed its gate; its file is published as a result (owner's decision of 15/11/2026). The ninth, s6-sleep, stopped at its pilot, so its nano model was never trained. A tenth pre-registration trained nothing: s6-route fuses the s6-route-nano file with a static embedding, and passed its gate on fresh slates, with a system defined after v2's test was read. The s6-pico ablation only chose the recipe of the others, and its rules adopted none of the s6 additions (Muon, Parallax, XSA and the rest): there is no s6-pico model to ship. Every number is on the [results page](/results/#s6-november-2026) and in [`BENCH.md`](https://github.com/maxgfr/skycmd/blob/main/BENCH.md).

## Reproduce

```bash
# Closed-loop games and native latency per decision, 30 episodes per game (seed 7)
cargo run --release -p sky-arena -- site/public/models/s3-pico.gguf --episodes 30 \
  --game flappy,rescue,dodge,doom --json runs/ship-s3-pico.arena.json

# Native latency of one model on a Flappy frame (sky-bench)
export CARGO_TARGET_DIR=$PWD/target/sky-infer
cargo run --release -p sky-infer --bin sky-bench -- site/public/models/s3-pico.gguf --questions 2 --iters 4000

# WASM latency per Flappy frame under Node (the modules of the Play page: SIMD128 by default,
# --relaxed for the relaxed-SIMD build)
scripts/build-wasm.sh
node crates/sky-web/bench/bench-node.mjs site/public/models/*.gguf
node crates/sky-web/bench/bench-node.mjs --relaxed site/public/models/*.gguf
```

The numbers in `site/public/bench.json` come from these measurements (its `note` field says which). `scripts/export-models.sh` regenerates it only for s1 models in the Laya layout, so do not run it to refresh this page: it would drop s3-pico and s2-nano.

The in-browser button loads each published model and times 300 decisions with the same wasm build as the Play page: relaxed SIMD where the browser supports it, else SIMD128 (the line under the button says which). The WASM numbers of the table come from `site/public/bench.json` and were measured with the SIMD128 build.
