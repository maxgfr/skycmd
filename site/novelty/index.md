**What it rests on.** None of the building blocks is new, and each is credited with a link on [Sources](/sources/#research-the-architecture-builds-on): a shared block looped with an exit after each pass (TRM, Ouro), one token per measured value (FT-Transformer, FoNE), ternary weights trained in (BitNet b1.58), DAgger, temperature scaling, conformal risk control, and the teachers and backbones (GLM-5.3-flash, Ettin). What is ours is the combination, the scale, the closed-loop checks and the published measurements, failures included.


**In short:** a System One decision model, the kind of model Jev, Laya and Julia-1 introduced in September 2026, fits in **251 KB**. It was trained **from scratch in under an hour** on a laptop and plays four control games **at the level of an exact oracle**, in about **50 µs per frame in a web browser**. Around it, skycmd brings a decision format that is invariant to the order and names of the options by construction, a calibration lesson for early exits, experiments whose criteria were fixed before any test, and a whole chain in Rust that runs in the browser. As far as we could find, nothing this small exists in this format. Training and inference are Rust; a few gate scripts, the s4 teacher and some baselines are Python.

**Told as stories.** Four of these lines are also written up for a wider audience in [Articles](/articles/): [the tool router](/articles/a-3-7-mb-tool-router) (two failures, then a pass and its limits, then the versions that closed the line), [the secrets verifier](/articles/a-250-kb-secrets-verifier) (rules propose, the model judges), [the end-of-turn detector](/articles/a-632-kb-end-of-turn-detector) (one failure, one harness bug, then a pass) and its [third version, trained on real conversations](/articles/an-end-of-turn-detector-trained-on-real-conversations) (labels checked before training, and a gain that did not reach voice agents) and [the memory](/articles/a-memory-that-works-and-a-model-that-does-not) (a memory that is used, and a gate that fails); and [an overview](/articles/what-worked-what-failed-on-one-laptop) tells the whole line of experiments as of 19 November 2026. ## 1. A decision model 40× to 300× smaller

Every open decision model we found starts from a pretrained backbone of at least 17M parameters. [s3-pico](/tutorial/21-s3-recursive-tiny) was trained from zero, and it still answers typed questions (choice / score / noul) with calibrated probabilities in one forward pass.

| | skycmd s3-pico | smallest public model in the same format | typical decision models |
|---|---|---|---|
| parameters | **445,345** | 17M ([oscar-1-17m](https://huggingface.co/mgoeckel/oscar-1-17m)) | 144M ([Julia-1](https://huggingface.co/SupersonicLabs/Julia-1)) to 27B |
| file | **251 KB** (ternary) | ~37 MB | hundreds of MB to tens of GB |
| training | **from scratch, ~47 min on an Apple M5** | fine-tuned from a pretrained encoder | fine-tuned from large pretrained models |
| per decision | **21 µs native, ~50 µs per frame in WASM** | milliseconds | milliseconds to seconds (Jev: 70–500 ms, vendor figure) |

What s3-pico does with that budget, over 30 seeded episodes per game, with the shipped file (`runs/ship-s3-pico.arena.json`):

- **Flappy Drone:** 200/200 pipes, 0 crashes.
- **Rescue Run:** 100% of hikers rescued.
- **Obstacle Dodge:** 87% of courses completed, exactly the oracle's rate. The coded safety reflex had to step in only 14 times, against 1,026 for our first model.
- **Doom-like:** 100% of levels completed and the same kill rate as the oracle, with no deaths.
- **Depth used:** 1.05 to 1.73 loops out of 4, thanks to early exits.

You can play against it on the [Play](/play/) page.

## 2. An architecture designed for decisions in a loop

Each piece below is known on its own; the combination is ours.

- **Two streams with a cache:** the question and the options are encoded once and cached, and each frame encodes only the state.
- **Options never see each other,** so the answer cannot depend on their order. This holds by construction, not by augmentation.
- **One token per measured value:** a pair such as `vy -3` becomes a single token, combining FT-Transformer and FoNE.
- **Depth from recursion, not from parameters:** one shared block is looped four times, with an exit after every loop, as in TRM and Ouro.
- **Ternary weights trained in** with QAT, BitNet-style.

## 3. A lesson: calibrate early exits where the model is used

- Early-exit thresholds are normally fitted on i.i.d. validation questions. Fitted that way (τ = 0.62 / 0.62 / 0.69 for choice / score / noul), **s2-nano failed** in closed loop: over 5 arena seeds × 30 episodes it crashed in every Flappy episode (15.9 ± 2.3 pipes of 200, 30 crashes of 30) and needed 1,931 ± 1,106 reflex vetoes in Dodge. Confident shallow exits were wrong in the critical states.
- Calibrated **by playing the games** (τ = 0.95), the same weights flew 191.9 ± 7.2 pipes (2.4 ± 2.1 crashes of 30), matched the oracle in Rescue and came within 0.1 of it in Dodge and Doom (Doom is played by s2-nano-doom, its Doom fine-tune, as on the site), at 3.2 to 3.4 of 4 layers in Flappy, Rescue and Dodge, and 4.0 in Doom (s2-nano-doom).
- s3-pico, a different model, ships with a τ chosen in closed loop (0.6) and uses about 1.4 of its 4 loops (1.44 Flappy, 1.57 Rescue, 1.05 Dodge, 1.73 Doom). There the threshold barely matters: τ = 0.6, τ = 0.5 and full depth give the same scores and crashes on all four games and all five seeds (Dodge vetoes 9.6 against 9.8 at τ = 0.5). For s3-pico, the closed-loop calibration confirmed that shallow exits were safe rather than rescuing anything.
- So the evidence for the lesson is one model, s2-nano, on five seeds. We found little prior work on calibrating early exits in closed-loop control, and we think it is worth checking on any early-exit model that drives something; this data does not show that it is a general rule. Numbers: [`loopcal.json`](/results/loopcal.json), produced by `cargo xtask loopcal`.

A related recipe: **imitation that holds up in closed loop.** With DAgger, the model plays and the oracle labels the states it reaches. It turned "crashes before the first pipe" into "200/200" in two to three rounds of 8 minutes each ([DAgger chapter](/tutorial/20-dagger-lessons)).

## 4. Decisions that cannot depend on the order or names of the options

The s5 iso format ([chapter 23](/tutorial/23-s5-lite)) gives these properties by construction. Each one is checked by an exact test, natively and in WASM, so none of them is an experimental result:

- permuting the options changes nothing;
- renaming the option keys changes nothing, since the model reads the definitions only (this removes the yes/no swap of 2609.26758);
- s + n = 1 when a negation only swaps the two definitions;
- the state encoding does not depend on the question, so it is cached per state.

At equal data, the same encoder was much more accurate in this format than in the usual one on the fitted bench: 0.674 against 0.429 (+0.245, 95% CI [+0.136, +0.321]). It was less well calibrated, though, and the calibration margin fixed in advance failed (see below).

## 5. Experiments you can check, failures included

- **Criteria fixed before any test.** The s5 [pre-registration](https://github.com/maxgfr/skycmd/blob/main/docs/s5-prereg.md) was committed first. Every amendment is dated and says what had been seen when it was written.
- **The test split is locked by the tooling.** The evaluation tool refuses the test split of a frozen set unless the pre-registration lists the set's hash, and it logs every test pass.
- **Failures are published like successes.** s4 missed its gate, and the three pre-registered s5 bets each missed their criterion. The online escalation bet failed in all six cells. The [Results](/results/) page shows each of them with its interval and the reason.

## 6. The whole chain in Rust, down to the browser

- data from Hugging Face;
- training with [mlx-rs](https://github.com/oxiglade/mlx-rs) on Apple Silicon;
- a GGUF writer, with f32, q8_0 and Q4_0 exports;
- an inference engine compiled to WebAssembly, with SIMD kernels for 8-bit and 4-bit weights.

The same file runs in the browser, in a CLI that speaks the `/v1/systemone` API, in Node and in Python (built from source). Our s1 models also run in llama.cpp and wllama. The s5 model takes 21 MB in q8_0 and 12 MB in Q4_0, against 70 MB in f32.

The [Live](/live/) page runs everything on your machine, with no server: the decision models, and a cascade replay in which s5 answers alone or escalates to an auditor that also audits a random share of the accepted answers.

## 7. Methods worth reusing, even from a failed model

[s4-micro](/tutorial/22-s4-generic-decisions) tried to make the architecture generic by distilling Qwen3.5-4B in one night. It missed its gate (0.343 on the typed-decisions bench, against a target of 0.55), but three of its methods are worth reusing:

- **A scoring teacher with per-group bias calibration.** The 4B never generates: one prefill, the logits of the option letters only. A temperature and a bias per option are then fitted per (source, type, number of options) against the gold labels, which removed a strong "true" bias on nouls.
- **Randomized label sets with abstention.** Options are shuffled, distractors are added, an abstention option is appended, and in 12 % of the records the gold option is removed so that "I don't know" is the target.
- **Deep improvement supervision on early exits.** Each exit trains toward the target raised to a power that grows from 0.5 to 1, so the early exits learn a softer copy of the target.

Its failure is a lesson too: without language pretraining, loops cannot make up for it. A BERT-Tiny of 4.4M parameters fine-tuned on the same data reaches 0.794 on Banking77, against 0.303 for s4.

## 8. What s6 adds (trained in November 2026)

Each s6 model has a pre-registration with its gate. The five of the big run are trained, and so are the second version of s6-micro, s6-secrets-verify-pico, s6-route-nano, s6-ear2-nano, s6-sleep3-nano, s6-cast2-pico, s6-route7-nano and s6-ear3-nano; s6-sleep stopped at its pilot in its first version and at a CPU check in its second, its third trained s6-sleep3-nano, and its fourth failed its first check, K3, on 18 November and its second, K1, on 19 November, which closed it with no model; s6-route trains nothing and fuses s6-route-nano's file with a static embedding, and its fourth to sixth versions stopped at their data checks before any training. Their numbers are on the [Results](/results/#s6-november-2026) page and in [`BENCH.md`](https://github.com/maxgfr/skycmd/blob/main/BENCH.md), and the points below come from there; the run that trained them has a [final report](https://github.com/maxgfr/skycmd/blob/main/docs/big-run-report.md).

**What the trained models show.**
- **One 715 KB file for the games and for questions in free text.** s6-nano (1,486,225 ternary parameters) plays all four games at the oracle's score and reaches 0.480 on `typed_decisions_bench`, above the majority class (0.398). The earlier small models stayed below it: s3-pico 0.382 and s4-micro 0.352 on the same records. Its ECE over all the test sources is 0.0479. That clears its gate as written, and the gate was too low: a constant answer per task passes it, since answering each question with its most common training answer scores 0.4815, and on 17 of the bench's 20 tasks s6-nano gives one answer to at least 95% of the items ([study](https://github.com/maxgfr/skycmd/blob/main/docs/s6-nano2-study.md)). It runs in this browser on [Live](/live/#s6-nano-the-questions-it-was-trained-for) and [Play](/play/), and its WASM answers match the native engine to 9.5e-7 on 200 bench questions.
- **No architectural change was adopted.** The pre-registered pico ablation adopted none of the additions listed below. Each needed a validation gain of 0.01 (0.005 for the training options) with no game lost: Muon brought 0.0051 and lost Flappy score, Parallax 0.0054, XSA 0.0081, and SODA, exit distillation, the EMA, StableQAT and the curriculum all lowered accuracy (`runs/recipe/recipe.json`). This is the outcome the literature warned about: gains over AdamW shrink once AdamW is tuned as carefully ([Fantastic Pretraining Optimizers](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.02046)), and at equal time AdamW can beat Muon on small language models ([When Does Muon Actually Win?](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.21555)). What changed from s4-micro is the pretraining: 0.87B tokens, 588 per parameter, against 30 minutes on 150 MB for s4-micro. An MLM encoder wants far more tokens per parameter than a decoder (Training compute-optimal transformer encoder models, EMNLP 2025). The GLM-5.3-flash teacher, the tokenizer and the 512-token windows changed too, and no ablation separates these causes.
- **A guarantee checked by playing, and rejected.** Conformal risk control (α = 0.02; [Fast yet Safe](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.20915), CALM at NeurIPS 2022) cut s6-nano's depth to 1.01 to 1.83 of its 4 passes per game. In closed loop, Rescue fell to 93 % and the Dodge vetoes rose from 23 to 1,016. The pre-registered rule allows 2 %, so the model ships at full depth. The guarantee holds on held-out questions, not on the states that playing with early exits leads to: the lesson of section 3, met again with a better tool.
- **A 250 KB secret detector that beats a rule scanner on file types it never saw, on its own generator's bench, and fails on real files.** s6-secrets-pico (445,345 ternary parameters, the s3-pico graph with a 320-token state) reads a short text and answers four typed questions with the same engine as the games. On its synthetic bench of docker-compose, toml, go and ini files, none of them in its training, it scores 0.9990 on "is a usable secret exposed?" and 0.9795 on "which service issued it?", where a gitleaks-style rule scanner scores 0.977 and 0.787 on the same texts. It is right on all 46 texts the scanner gets wrong, the random values under secret-sounding names among them. Its line answer is weak (0.6859, right on 383 of the 1,010 texts that expose a secret). And the bench is synthetic: measured after publication on 719 real source, configuration and documentation files in which a rule scanner finds nothing, cut into 22,433 chunks of at most 7 lines and 300 characters, it flags 10.6% of the chunks and 59.8% of the files at p ≥ 0.9, and 5.9% and 49.4% at p ≥ 0.999. The flagged chunks are ordinary text (imports, prose, JSON), and its probabilities are not calibrated outside the generator ([real text](/results/#s6-secrets-real-text), [measurement](https://github.com/maxgfr/secretgate/pull/19)). The gate stands; the model stays published as the result of its pre-registration, not as a tool. It runs on its bench texts on [Live](/live/#s6-secrets-pico-is-a-secret-exposed-in-this-text), about 1.4 ms per decision in WASM under Node.
- **A failed router and a static embedding, fused, pass on fresh slates.** s6-route trains nothing: it multiplies, option by option, the probabilities of the s6-route-nano file and of potion-base-2M packed to 4 bits, raised to 0.81 and renormalised, and asks the user below a top probability of 0.671047; 3.7 MB in two files. The fusion was first seen on v2's already-read test, so it was pre-registered with its parameters from validation and read once on fresh requests ([pre-registration](https://github.com/maxgfr/skycmd/blob/main/docs/s6-nano3-prereg.md)). On 945 fresh requests about tools never seen in training it reaches 0.8868, against 0.8169 for BM25 and 0.9048 for all-MiniLM-L6-v2 (91.6 MB), with an ECE of 0.0162 and 30.9 ms per request in WASM: every condition of its gate held. These requests are about v2's held-out test tools, the latency bar was set after v2 was measured, and the advantage over BM25 leans on the three largest clusters of the test (without them +0.0202 [−0.0062, +0.0481]); with tool names masked it falls to 0.2497 ([results](/results/#s6-route-passed), [Router](/router/)). A diagnosis made after the gate, on validation files only, found why: in training the right tool of every renamed slate was the only option whose description the teacher had rewritten, and *none* was never right there, so under `tool_<k>` names the model part picks the description that reads most like a rewrite and almost never answers *none*; no number above changes ([diagnosis](https://github.com/maxgfr/skycmd/blob/main/docs/s6-route4-diagnosis.md)). A retraining without that shortcut (v4) stopped at its data checks on 18 November, before any training: its hard slates, which hold v2's tools, let a rule that never reads the request pick the right tool above chance (the shortest name on 0.4219 against 0.3721 in the intents group), so the published model part was trained on such cues, whose use is not measured ([result](https://github.com/maxgfr/skycmd/blob/main/docs/s6-route4-prereg.md)). A fifth version, with every drawn distractor matched to the right tool's surface, stopped at its data checks the same day: v4's cues were gone, smaller ones of +0.03 to +0.08 above chance remained, and in When2Call's slates *none* could be read from the spread of the options ([result](https://github.com/maxgfr/skycmd/blob/main/docs/s6-route5-prereg.md)). After the fix, *none* could still be read from the spread of the options, by more than before (+0.38 above its share, against +0.24). The line is closed, with no eighth version, and s6-route stays the published router; on those requests it reads 0.5530, reported ([results](/results/#s6-route7-nano-failed), [result block](https://github.com/maxgfr/skycmd/blob/main/docs/s6-route7-prereg.md)).
- **One task and a teacher beat three tasks and a pretraining: the end-of-turn detector passes where s6-ear-nano failed.** s6-ear2-nano (s6-ear v2) drops the codec pretraining and the keywords, and trains one task for 240 working minutes as a student of Smart Turn v3.2 (BSD-2): a dedicated `turn` graph of 1,306,178 ternary parameters (632 KB, `skycmd-turn`), a front end of 40 log-mel bands plus pitch, voicing and energy per 10 ms hop, and the last 4 s of audio. Its rung was fixed by a latency probe (2.39 ms in WASM) and its harness checked against Pipecat's published number before the test was read. On the 31,527 test clips of Smart Turn v3.2 it answers 0.8993 (bar 0.85; v1 0.5681, the silence timer 0.4979, the teacher 0.9260), with a false cut-off of 0.0847 and an END recall of 0.8627 at its validation threshold τ₈₅ = 0.7244, and 1.40 ms per decision natively. Take it when size and speed matter: 2.7 points of accuracy given up for 0.63 MB instead of 8.7 MB and about 2.4 ms in WASM instead of about 32 ms. Said with the pass: on real conversational cuts (eot-bench, which the design never read) it and its teacher are far weaker (AUC 0.7299 against 0.7838), and at τ₈₅ it answers "finished" on 0.5253 of the real ends of turn; it is weakest on Vietnamese, Marathi and Bengali; and the test set is partly in-sample for its design choices, though never for its weights ([results](/results/#s6-ear2-nano-passed)).
- **Real conversations taught the detector its own corpus, not speech addressed to an agent.** s6-ear3-nano (s6-ear v3) keeps v2's graph and trains it again from v2's weights for 120 working minutes, half on real two-speaker English conversations (otoSpeech, gated, CC BY 4.0; each pause of one speaker labelled from the other speaker's channel: end of turn when the other takes the floor within 4 s, hold when the same speaker resumes first) cut where the demo's policy asks, half on Smart Turn's clips extended by their own background. Every amendment is dated and says what had been seen when it was written.

What follows is how the code differs, and why. The trained models use the data, the teacher and the conformal tool; the encoder and optimizer additions stay in the code, unadopted.

**A better encoder for the same weights.**
- **Parallax attention** (arXiv 2605.29157). Each head learns a probe that turns softmax attention, a local constant estimator, into a local linear one. It is one extra row block in the same fused projection, so the ternary storage and the first-block lookup table need no new tensor. With a zero probe it is softmax attention exactly, so the options stay isolated.
- **Muon v2.** Decoupled weight decay and an update scaled to AdamW's RMS (Kimi), then a two-stage Newton–Schulz iteration whose second stage pulls the singular values to within 0.5% of 1.
- **A pre-registered ablation** decides what ships, one change at a time: Parallax, exclusive self-attention, SODA weight decay, exit self-distillation, a weight EMA, the StableQAT gradient and a pretraining curriculum.

**Early exits with a guarantee.** The exit thresholds are chosen by conformal risk control: an early answer disagrees with full depth at most α of the time. The threshold is then checked by playing at it, because playing with early exits changes which states the model visits. The guarantee does not survive that change unchecked; this is the lesson of section 3, turned into a tool.

**A teacher that never heats the laptop.** GLM-5.3-flash, open weights under MIT, labels all 747,198 training decisions through the z.ai API, with four workers.
- **Resumable:** labelling restarts where it stopped, across the 5-hour usage windows.
- **Moderation-proof:** a batch refused by the moderation is halved until the refused text stands alone, so one sensitive text costs its neighbours nothing.
- **Filtered:** only the labels that agree with the gold answer are mixed in.

**Small specialists on the same engine.**
- **s6-cast:** a probabilistic forecaster on the same encoder. Its first run, s6-cast-pico (369,792 parameters), failed its gate; its file is published as a result (owner's decision of 15/11/2026) (above). Its second, s6-cast2-pico (377,568 parameters, a computed-periodicity front end), failed its gate too and is published the same way; the line stops there (above).
- **s6-secrets:** API-key detection. Its synthetic keys follow each provider's public format, and its bench uses file types the training never shows. The bar is a gitleaks-style rule scanner, and s6-secrets-pico clears it on the bench, but not on real files (above). Its second version judges rule candidates on real repositories and fails G1 ([results](/results/#s6-secrets-verify-pico-failed)).

**System 1 first, System 2 only when needed.** The same idea as the early exits, one level up. For LFM3-1.7B, a 6-coefficient confidence probe decides, question by question, whether the model should think before answering. Thinking can make a small model abandon a good first answer. **Run on 14 and 15 November 2026, it failed its pre-registered criteria** ([results](/results/#lfm3-s1s2)): on 1,000 test questions (GSM8K and MATH-500), with the 1,024-token budget chosen on calibration, System 1 alone scores 0.667 at 559 tokens, System 2 alone 0.796 at 958, the router 0.786 at 1,032 and the oracle 0.814. The router keeps the accuracy (H2, yes) but costs more than always thinking (H3, compute halved: no), and does not beat both modes (H4: no). Thinking helps this model in maths, and the answer without thinking is not cheap enough for routing to pay. The fine-tuning that was to make System 1 cheaper trained nothing: on 18 November no LoRA method allowed by its pre-registration ended an optimizer step on this 16 GB Mac within the memory bound set before the run, so by its own rule nothing was trained ([amendment 7](https://github.com/maxgfr/skycmd/blob/main/docs/lfm3-ft-prereg.md)). On 19 November the owner decided not to rent compute for it, and the fine-tuning is closed: there are no weights (amendment 8).

**s6-ear: a decision model that listens** ([chapter 26](/tutorial/26-s6-ear)).
- **Outcome of the first version: it failed three of its four gates** ([results](/results/#s6-ear-nano-failed)). End of turn 0.5681 against 0.9263 for Smart Turn v3.2 on the same clips; 12 Speech Commands keywords 0.8885 against a bar of 0.90; zero-shot keywords 0.2100 in English (the one gate that held, bar 0.20) and 0.1330 in French. Its control, trained the same way without the Mimi pretraining, is more accurate on all four sets. The points below describe the design that was tried, and say which parts of it ran. The second version, a small student of Smart Turn for end of turn only, passed its gate on 17/11/2026 and is published as [s6-ear2-nano](/results/#s6-ear2-nano-passed) (section 8, "What the trained models show"). The third version, trained on real two-speaker conversations ([`docs/s6-ear3-prereg.md`](https://github.com/maxgfr/skycmd/blob/main/docs/s6-ear3-prereg.md)), failed its gate on 19/11/2026 and is published as [s6-ear3-nano](/results/#s6-ear3-nano-failed), as a result (section 8).
- **Mimi without Mimi** (ran). Kyutai's semantic speech tokens, without the 48M-parameter codec. A 1.5M model (1,532,449 parameters) reads log-mel frames at Mimi's own frame rate (80 ms) and learns, by masked prediction, the Mimi codebook-0 codes Kyutai published for LibriSpeech. Mimi never runs. The pretraining learned its own task (codebook-0 accuracy 0.2196 on masked validation frames) and did not help the decisions: the control without it did better.
- **The question is text, the state is sound** (ran). The options are words, so it can be asked about words it never heard in training. Its zero-shot test holds out 50 MSWC words per language, in English and French: the 0.2100 and 0.1330 above.
- **Designed, not run.** Two parts of the design were left for a later version and never ran: the cross-modal distillation, in which GLM-5.3-flash labels the LibriSpeech transcripts and s6-ear learns those answers from the audio (no transcript was labelled), and a voice agent's System 1 (every 80 ms: keep listening, acknowledge, speak or yield, from AMI's speaker timings; the AMI downloader was not written). v1 was trained and gated on end of turn and keywords only.
- **Latency, measured on the smoke run's model** (a few minutes of training on 200 clips per set, before the real run): 5 ms per decision for 10 s of audio natively, 0.67 ms in WASM for 1 s clips. The browser front-end matches the native one bit for bit, because its log uses no libm. The trained v1, read afterwards from its run files, takes 3,435 µs per decision natively (median over all test sources) and 6,696 µs in WASM (median over 20 test clips) ([final report](https://github.com/maxgfr/skycmd/blob/main/docs/big-run-report.md)); v2 takes 2.39 ms in WASM.
- **Honest bars.** The end-of-turn gate is Smart Turn v3.2 (8M parameters, five times larger) on Pipecat's official held-out test set. We first planned a split of Smart Turn's own training data, and dropped it before any run.

## What it does not bring (honestly)

- **None of the building blocks is new.** The novelty is in how they are combined, in the scale and in the closed-loop calibration lesson. Our literature search was two research passes, not a systematic review.
- **The games are friendly.** Code measures the useful features (relative clearances, an A* waypoint) and the model decides, following the [jev-drone](https://github.com/RomanSlack/jev-drone) philosophy. A small MLP on the same features would be a fair baseline, and it was not run.
- **The tiny model is a specialist.** On general decisions it is modest: 0.54 on our held-out mix and 0.38 on the external typed-decisions benchmark, against about 0.73 for the 144M Julia-1.
- **No pre-registered claim for s5.** None of the three bets is claimed: the iso format missed its calibration margin, the coherence losses improved only the identities they were trained on, and 8-bit quantization could not be shown non-inferior for lack of power.
- **s5 is a small English model.** There is no French version.
- **The results are not peer-reviewed.** They come from our own benchmarks, with single runs for s3 and s4, three seeds for s5, and one training run per gated s6 model except s6-sleep3-nano (three training seeds, pooled).
- **Limits that apply to every result.** Except s5 (three seeds per format) and s6-sleep3-nano (three training seeds, pooled), every gated model was trained once, on one seed. The intervals are bootstraps over test items (questions, episodes, clips, slates, repositories), not over training runs: they say how much a number would move on other test items, not how much it would move if the same recipe were trained again. The one line whose checks were read on two training seeds, s6-sleep v4, failed K3 on both and met K1's bar on one seed of the two. Some passes are second looks, on test records already read when the system was designed (s6-micro v2); some gates were read on a benchmark the project built itself (s6-secrets-pico's synthetic bench) or on a test set that the design of an earlier version had already seen (s6-ear2-nano, s6-ear3-nano, s6-cast2-pico); each model's section says which. The numbers are on [Results](/results/).
- **s6-secrets-pico is not a scanner.** Its bench is synthetic (our formats, file templates and class shares, fake keys with random bodies). On real files with no secret it flags 59.8% of the files at p ≥ 0.9 and 49.4% at p ≥ 0.999; its recall on real secrets was not measured. It cannot be trusted to locate a leak either: line 0.6859, and confident when wrong (ECE 0.3051). Its second version, s6-secrets-verify-pico, failed its gate; its file is published as a result (owner's decision of 15/11/2026), and it is not a scanner either, so skycmd offers no secret scanner.
- **s6-nano is a modest generalist, and on most bench tasks it does not read the state.** 0.480 on `typed_decisions_bench` clears the majority class, but s5-lite reaches 0.696 and oscar-1-17m 0.6775, and answering each question with its most common training answer scores 0.4815 (s6-nano 0.4805). On 17 of the 20 tasks it gives one answer to at least 95% of the items. The bench's tasks were 0.4% of what it drew in decision training. A linear model of at most 715,136 bytes reaches 0.703 on the validation split of the same tasks, and 0.947 / 0.944 on MASSIVE English / French validation once trained on MASSIVE (a rehearsal on validation, not a test). Its French training data are two yes/no tasks; on When2Call, `tool_call` is 13.9% of the training split and 35% of the test, and it predicts it 39 times in 2,000; NOTAM has no training data ([study](https://github.com/maxgfr/skycmd/blob/main/docs/s6-nano2-study.md)). It fails on NOTAM (0.055 on the subject question) and stays at 0.404 on When2Call. Its English MASSIVE score is not strictly zero-shot: 206 of the 2,000 test utterances also appear in the training split, 201 of them in a source derived from MASSIVE.

## The technical report, and what a peer-reviewed version would still need

The project is written up as a [technical report](/paper/), dated 20 November 2026 and not peer-reviewed. A peer-reviewed version would still need:

- Ablations of s3 over several seeds: fused values, recursion, static cache, DAgger.
- A standard control benchmark with vector states.
- Baselines: a same-size MLP on the games, an RL policy, oscar-1-17m.
- The s5 baselines B1 (label log-probability through the MLM head) and B3 (a linear head on the frozen backbone); see [Not done yet](/results/#not-done-yet).
- A systematic literature review.

Already done: closed-loop vs i.i.d. early-exit calibration over 5 arena seeds (point 3, `cargo xtask loopcal`). The natural first target is still a short workshop paper: *closed-loop calibration of early exits for tiny decision models*, with skycmd as the open-source demonstrator.

See also the [tutorial](/tutorial/), the [night experiments](https://github.com/maxgfr/skycmd/blob/main/docs/night-experiments.md) and the [sources](/sources/).
